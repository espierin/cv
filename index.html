Profile

Diagnostics, science, and implementation in one professional profile

Eric Spierings is an Associate Professor and medical immunologist at UMC Utrecht. He leads transplantation diagnostics within the Central Diagnostic Laboratory and works at the interface of routine care, translational immunology, immunogenetics, and clinical data innovation.

My mission is to ensure that every transplant patient achieves the best possible outcome by embedding advanced immunogenetic matching and innovative diagnostics into daily clinical practice.

His scientific work addresses how donor-recipient immunogenetic differences shape allo-immunity and transplant outcome. This includes HLA typing, antibody diagnostics, indirect HLA epitope recognition, PIRCHE-based risk assessment, donor-specific antibodies, graft rejection, graft failure, and immune monitoring after transplantation.

A consistent principle across this work is that novel analytical methods should move beyond proof of concept. They must be interpretable, technically robust, compatible with laboratory quality systems, and connected to clinical workflows.

Scientific focus

Four connected fields of work

His focus combines high-quality transplantation diagnostics with mechanistic research, computational matching, and implementation into clinical and laboratory systems.

01

HLA diagnostics

Accredited HLA typing and antibody diagnostics, including clinical interpretation, donor-recipient compatibility assessment, donor-specific antibody evaluation, and direct consultation with transplant teams.

02

Epitope matching

Quantifying immunological compatibility through indirect T-cell epitope recognition, PIRCHE-II, B-cell epitope concepts, and combined risk models for rejection and graft outcome.

03

Data standards

Developing interoperable representations for HLA and antibody data, including GL String, HML, HAML, and related structures that support reproducible exchange between laboratories, registries, software, and research systems.

04

Clinical implementation

Translating assays, algorithms, and data models into validated workflows, interpretable reports, decision support, and infrastructure for routine transplantation care.
